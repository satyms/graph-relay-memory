## 2. Tracks

You may enter one, two or all three. Each track has its own prize. **You must name every track you are entering when you submit.**

| Track | Focus | Key focus areas |
|---|---|---|
| 01 — Agents That Act on Connected Data | An agent that uses the graph to decide and act (fraud, security triage, codebases) | Multi-hop reasoning; graph queries and algorithms as agent tools; tool selection through MCP; actions based on what the graph shows; explainable results |
| 02 — Agent Memory and Coordination | Agents that remember across sessions and share state | Episodic, semantic and procedural memory; persistent conversation history; shared state and handoffs; high-concurrency reads and writes; separate memory per user or tenant |
| 03 — Company Brain | An agent that understands how a company works | Docs, tickets and chats in one graph; who owns and knows what; tracing decisions to their source; keeping knowledge current; answers with clear sources |

## 3. Official rules

### Eligibility and teams
1. Participate solo or in a team of up to 4 members. Each participant may be on only one team.
2. Open to developers worldwide.

### Required technology
3. FalkorDB must be the primary graph database, and it must support a central part of what the product does.
4. **The graph has to do real work.** A visualisation drawn on top of ordinary storage does not qualify. If the product still works once FalkorDB is taken out, the graph is decoration and the project does not meet the requirement.
5. Run FalkorDB however suits you: Docker, a self-hosted instance, or FalkorDB Cloud. Any language, framework, frontend or deployment platform may be used alongside it.

### Scope
6. The challenge is open-ended within the three tracks (see section 2).

### Data
7. Use only data you are allowed to use: public, synthetic, or your own.
8. Do **not** load private, login-protected, paywalled, personal, or otherwise restricted information into your graph.

### Timeline and originality
9. The main coding and design work must begin after the hackathon starts. Before the start you may discuss ideas, create notes, sketch the graph model, or prepare diagrams.
10. You may use frameworks, open-source libraries, public APIs, templates, third-party tools, and publicly available datasets and assets. The original work completed during the hackathon is what gets judged.
11. Existing projects: general libraries, templates and infrastructure may be reused, but the submission and its main FalkorDB implementation must be new work completed during the event.

### Submission requirements
12. Every submission must include:
    - A public source-code repository
    - A README with setup instructions **and your graph data model**
    - The Cypher queries or graph algorithms the product depends on
    - A demo video showing the working project
    - A live deployment, or complete local setup instructions if it cannot be deployed
    - The track(s) you are entering
13. A live deployment is preferred. Projects that cannot be deployed must include complete local setup instructions and a working demo video.
14. Submissions close at the end of the event window (Oct 18, 11:59 PM IST).

### AI use
15. AI coding assistants are allowed, but their use **must be disclosed**.
16. Participants must understand the submitted code and be able to explain the graph model, the queries, the project architecture, and the technical decisions behind them. You remain responsible for the code, architecture, security and correctness.
17. Projects that are entirely generated by AI without meaningful participant contribution, verification or technical understanding may be rejected.

### Prizes and ownership
18. One prize per track. Every member of a winning team receives the prize.
19. Intellectual property developed during the hackathon belongs to the participant or team that created it. Teams are encouraged to agree on ownership internally before submitting.

### Conduct
20. Treat participants, organisers, sponsors, speakers, judges and community members with respect.
21. Harassment, discrimination, plagiarism, or attempts to manipulate the judging process will result in disqualification.
22. Failure to follow these rules or the Code of Conduct may result in disqualification.

## 6. What this means for our project (our interpretation, not official wording)

- **FalkorDB only.** Earlier suggestions in our planning that mentioned Neo4j do not apply. The design must run on FalkorDB, and FalkorDB's Cypher support should be checked for any query pattern we plan to rely on.
- **Tenant isolation fits the platform.** FalkorDB can host many isolated graphs in one instance, so one graph per tenant or user is a natural fit for Track 02's "separate memory per user or tenant".
- **Graph must be load-bearing.** Memory, claims, handoffs and facts should live as nodes and edges that agents read and write on every step. A vector store with a graph picture on top fails rule 4.
- **Synthetic data is fine,** and avoids the restricted-data rule. Do not ingest real private chats, tickets or personal documents.
- **Disclose AI assistants** in the README and be ready to walk through the code.
- **Time is short.** As of Oct 17 the window closes in roughly one day, so scope for a working demo, a clear README, and the video.
